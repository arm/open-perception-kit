# OPK: DMA-BUF, Vulkan preprocessing and ONNX GPU inference

Wednesday, 4 November 2026. About 90 seconds of overview and 30 seconds for results.

**Overview**

Originally, the OpChain prepared images, ran inference and interpreted results on the CPU. DMA-backed video required a copying bridge before processing.

 - DMA-BUF support now lets the chain preserve the video allocation. Mapping is deferred into CPU address space until a CPU stage needs the pixels.
 - Vulkan preprocessing can resize, convert colours and normalize the image on the GPU.
 - GPU based inference is in an experimental branch
    - Only generated by AI out of curiosity as a proof of concept


**Results**
Separately, Vulkan preprocessing (image resizing and cropping) takes 0.439 milliseconds against 0.840 on the CPU, but loading the data to the GPU and than back to CPU is more than 0.5ms, so putting the preprocessing alone does not really makes sense, this is why the GPU based inference was important (at least for me).

With the same FP32 models and real video, nano-320 improves from 20.90 to 34.27 frames per second. Small-640 improves from 3.28 to 6.50. All six tested models improve pipeline throughput by 53 to 99 percent. 

The experimental inference path loads the ONNX model through ONNX Runtime's native WebGPU provider. Dawn translates its work into Vulkan compute commands on the OPi6's Mali GPU.
